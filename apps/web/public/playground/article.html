How Lakeview cut its water use by 30% in two years

By Priya Raman · March 4, 2026 · 7 min read

When the Lakeview reservoir dropped to 41% capacity in the summer of 2023, the city council declared a water emergency for the first time in its 120-year history. Two years later, average household consumption has fallen from 310 litres per day to 215 litres, a drop of roughly 30%, while the population grew by 4%.

Smart meters first

The city replaced 48,000 mechanical meters with smart meters between January and October 2024, at a cost of $19.2 million funded by a state resilience grant. Residents can now see hourly usage in an app, and the utility sends an alert when a home uses water continuously for more than six hours, which usually means a leak. Leak alerts alone saved an estimated 1.1 billion litres in the first year.

Pricing that rewards saving

In April 2024 Lakeview moved to tiered pricing. The first 150 litres per person per day cost $1.10 per cubic metre; usage above that costs $3.40. Low-income households enrolled in the Lifeline programme receive the first tier free. Critics argued the change would hurt large families, so the council added a per-person allowance based on household size.

What comes next

The council will vote on May 12, 2026 on a $7 million plan to recycle greywater for park irrigation. Public comments are open until April 30. Officials say the reservoir is now at 68% and that the emergency status will be lifted if it stays above 60% through the summer.
